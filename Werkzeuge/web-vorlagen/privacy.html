Privacy.

Swiftly collects nothing. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no accounts with me. The app talks only to the Jellyfin server you enter yourself. Auf Deutsch

What stays on your device

  • Your server access token — in the iOS keychain, not in preferences. When you sign out it is deleted, and the server is asked to invalidate it.
  • Your settings — Direct Play, bitrate, audio and subtitle language, skip intervals, appearance.
  • The address of the server you last used, so you don't have to type it again.
  • A random device identifier. Jellyfin uses it to list your session in its dashboard. It is created on the device, says nothing about you, and leaves the device only towards your own server.
  • Cached posters and backdrops, so browsing doesn't reload every image.

All of it disappears when you delete the app.

What goes to your server

While playing, Swiftly reports to your Jellyfin server what is running and how far along it is. That is what makes "Continue watching" work and lets you pick up on another device. These reports go to your server — not to any service of mine, not to a third party.

What your server stores in turn is up to you, not to this app.

What I don't see

I run no service that Swiftly connects to. I cannot see that you use the app, what you watch, which server you have, or how often you open it. There are no crash reports to me, no statistics, no advertising identifiers.

Apple provides me, as it does for any app, with anonymous aggregate download figures. Individuals are not identifiable in them, and they come from Apple, not from the app.

Trailers

If a title has no trailer file on your server but carries a linked address, Swiftly opens that address in the browser — usually YouTube. From that moment the privacy policy of that site applies, not this one. This only happens if you tap "Trailer".

Children

Swiftly is not directed at children and collects no data — from anyone.

The website

swiftlyplayer.com is a handful of static files. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, loads no fonts, maps or scripts from other servers and stores nothing in your browser. There is no contact form and no newsletter.

Server logs

Like every web server, this one records that a file was fetched: IP address, time, the address requested, the amount of data, the browser identification and, where sent, the page you came from. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest is running the site and fending off attacks. These records are not combined with anything else, are not used to recognise you, and are deleted after a short time.

Hosting

The site is hosted by ALL-INKL.COM – Neue Medien Münnich, on servers in Germany. They process the log data on my behalf under a data processing agreement (Article 28 GDPR).

Links out

App Store, TestFlight, GitHub, Discord and jellyfin.org are other people's sites. Tapping one takes you there and their privacy notices apply. Nothing is sent to them before you do.

Payments on the supporter page

If you support Swiftly on the supporter page, with a monthly plan, a lifetime purchase or a tip, you pay through Link, a service by Stripe. For this purchase Link is the seller: it processes the payment, handles tax and sends the receipt. Your card or bank details go to Link and Stripe, not to me, and their privacy notices apply there. Stripe may process data outside the EU; that is covered by the safeguards in their notices.

The payment page itself, pay.paulherter.de, is run by me. When you start a payment it receives the amount and interval you chose, and like any web server it logs the request (IP address, time, browser identification). From the purchase I receive only the order: the plan, the amount, the time, your email address for the supporter key and, if you enter it, the name for the credits. The key is sent by email from noreply@paulherter.de. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(b) GDPR, carrying out what you asked for, and for the name in the credits your consent under Article 6(1)(a), which you can withdraw. Order records are kept as long as tax law requires. Nothing on the supporter page is tracked, and it sets no cookies.

Purchases in the apps

Where an app offers a tip or supporter purchase through the App Store or Google Play, Apple or Google is the seller. You pay them under their terms and privacy notices. I receive no data about buyers from the stores, only anonymous sales totals.

Supporter keys in the apps

A supporter key you enter in Swiftly Player on Linux or Windows is checked on the device. A lifetime key never leaves it. In the days before a monthly key runs out, the app asks pay.paulherter.de, at most twice a day, whether the plan still runs and fetches the renewed key; it sends only the random licence number from the key, nothing else about you or your device. Like any web server, the payment page logs that request (IP address, time). Legal basis is Article 6(1)(b) GDPR.
